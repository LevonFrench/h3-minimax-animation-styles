# Research — Early computer-graphics vector/low-poly animation

## Identification and provenance

The prompt intentionally blends eras of vector display, polygonal modeling, and early shading; no single historical system contains every trait.

## Historically and technically grounded mechanics

Black space, luminous vector grids, faceted low-polygon geometry, visible scanline or phosphor character, simple transforms, and hard Gouraud-like shading evoke early CG display limits.

## Actionable motion, material, and camera vocabulary

- Preserve material evidence, edge behavior, motivated overlap, readable spacing, and a stable endpoint.
- Keep topology coarse and transforms explicit; use grid, object rotation, and camera travel as separate readable systems rather than modern photoreal rendering.
- Camera vocabulary: steady forward grid dolly, 10% depth amplitude, constant speed; continuous screen direction; no unmotivated reframing.

## Prompt implications

Name visible causes, not style alone. Preserve the original subject/action, use legible intermediate states and coherent space, and reserve the final tail for a settled hold.
