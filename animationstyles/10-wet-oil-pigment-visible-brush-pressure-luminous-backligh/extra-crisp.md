# Title

Crimson Orchard Becomes a Stag

## Intent

Let wet pigment physically migrate into a new image, leaving material memory instead of a digital dissolve.

## Shot Class

Top-down glass-plane medium shot, 55 mm equivalent, locked camera.

## Subject / Continuity

Wet crimson and amber oil paint forms a single orchard tree on luminous glass. The same pigment mass must become one resting stag; faint prior-frame smears remain.

## Timed Action + Camera

100 BPM. Beat 1 (0.00-0.60): painted tree holds. Beats 2-3 (0.60-1.80): crown pigment is dragged downward into the stag's shoulders. Beats 4-5 (1.80-3.00): trunk is pushed laterally into torso and folded legs, brush-pressure ridges plainly visible. Beats 6-7 (3.00-4.20): two branches are repainted into antlers; soft edges migrate rather than dissolve. Beat 8 (4.20-4.80): one wiped highlight opens the eye. Hold the complete stag and locked camera through 5.1667 s.

## Spatial Block

Pigment remains within a 0.38 m glass field centered at (0,0,0). Camera fixed at (0,+0.72,0), straight down, no roll.

## Look + Lighting

Wet oil translucency, pigment drag, ridged brush pressure, wiped passages, luminous glass backlight. Amber backlight deepens gently while the eye is revealed.

## Sound

Silent visual take; no dialogue, music, foley, or soundtrack.

## Limits

One physical repainting action. No boat, sail, fish, cross-dissolve, liquefy effect, new pigment colors, visible hand, cuts, camera drift, text, logos, or endpoint motion.
