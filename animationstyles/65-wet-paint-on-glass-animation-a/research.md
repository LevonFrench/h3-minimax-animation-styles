# Research — Wet paint-on-glass animation

## Provenance and identification

This source is identical to folder 66. Paint-on-glass is an established under-camera technique in which wet pigment is altered and rephotographed frame by frame. The NFB explicitly documents Caroline Leaf's paint-on-glass practice alongside sand and scratch animation; the Academy guide lists painting on glass among alternative animation processes. Those sources support the technique, not this particular blue-sphere motif.

## Visual mechanics

- Retain wet, luminous pigment continuity by repainting rather than replacing the image with clean vector morphs.
- Circulate visible brush pressure and impasto around one bright central highlight.
- Allow controlled edge bleed, then visibly repaint the boundary to a circle.
- Keep the white ribbon optically distinct but materially painted, completing one orbit into the highlight.

## Prompt implications

Emphasize additive/subtractive brush action and frame-to-frame residue. Preserve the exact sphere, palette, and ring event; avoid claiming the source is a documented Caroline Leaf work.
