# Research — Wet paint-on-glass animation

## Provenance and identification

This source is textually identical to folder 65; there is no factual source-level distinction to invent. Paint-on-glass is an established under-camera technique based on changing wet pigment between exposures. The NFB documents Caroline Leaf's pioneering use of paint-on-glass, sand, and scratch methods, while the Academy identifies painting on glass as an alternative frame-by-frame process. These sources substantiate technique components, not the exact coined prompt label or blue-sphere design.

## Visual mechanics

- Keep persistent wet pigment, brush ridges, and residual mixing visible across frames.
- Make the blue circulation feel pushed through paint around a fixed luminous highlight.
- Use a deliberate subtract/repaint phase to recover the circular edge after controlled bleed.
- Resolve the single white ribbon back into the original highlight without a leftover seam.
