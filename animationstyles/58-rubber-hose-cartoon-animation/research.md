# Research — Rubber-hose cartoon elasticity

## Provenance and identification

“Rubber hose” conventionally describes early American cartoon figures with highly flexible, tube-like limbs and rhythmic elastic motion. MoMA's Fleischer program and its comparison of Disney, Iwerks, and Fleischer locate this visual language in 1930s studio animation. Applied here to a sphere, the actionable mechanics are squash-and-stretch, rhythmic pose clarity, monochrome contrast, and slight gate weave—not literal historical reproduction.

## Visual mechanics

- Use strong silhouette changes: pancake, tall oval, ripple, single axial spin.
- Preserve volume perceptually across squash and stretch.
- Snap graphic motion lines on accents, then remove them cleanly.
- Keep gate weave subtle and independent from the sphere's fixed center.

## Prompt implications

Order transformations so they do not overlap into mush. Treat the coined “sphere in rubber-hose styling” as a modern homage and avoid claiming archival authenticity.
