# Research: clay stop-motion hesitation and delayed follow-through

## Identification and provenance

This is a tactile plasticine stop-motion acting exercise. Physical pose increments, replacement facial pieces, practical miniature sets, anticipation, silhouette, and overlapping action communicate internal hesitation. Aardman material supports visible handwork and modeled-clay transformation; Disney names anticipation and secondary/follow-through action. The courier scenario is original.

## Visual and motion mechanics

- Split intention across body parts: hand nears handle while feet remain planted; head leans while torso stays back.
- Parcel becomes a shield, clarifying fear through prop staging.
- The backward compression loads the forward lunge.
- Parcel arriving one pose after torso is deliberate overlap, not continuity error.

## Prompt implications

Use a dark doorway and readable three-quarter profile. Hold hesitation long enough to understand listening and fear, then execute one quick entry. End on the empty/closing threshold rather than inventing an interior reveal.
